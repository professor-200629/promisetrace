# Commercial pilot hypothesis

Everything below is a **hypothesis to test**, not an observed result. No customers, revenue, savings or acquisition interest exist today.

## Who would pay

Independent digital-service sellers in India (and similar markets) who invoice overseas clients through PayPal several times a month — designers, developers, video editors — for fixed-scope jobs of roughly $100–$2,000 where scope is agreed in chat. Secondary: small agencies coordinating several freelancers.

## Workflow it replaces

Today: scope lives in WhatsApp/email; the seller sends a PayPal invoice or payment link; files go by Drive/WeTransfer; when something is disputed, the seller screenshots chats and digs up file links and transaction details by hand.

With PromiseTrace: one link the client approves before paying, a PayPal checkout tied to that version, delivery uploaded to the same place with automatic format/count/deadline checks, and an exportable record.

## Integration effort they would accept (assumption)

- Seller: under 10 minutes to set up; no code. Connects their own PayPal account (requires PayPal platform onboarding — not built).
- Client: no account friction beyond approving a brief and paying with PayPal as usual.

## What a pilot would measure

- **Activation**: share of pilot sellers who send at least 3 real jobs through it within 4 weeks.
- **Client acceptance**: share of clients who approve the brief before paying (vs. ignore the link).
- **Clarifications caught**: number of open questions resolved before payment, per job (from app data).
- **Disputes and time**: per seller, number of scope disagreements and minutes spent assembling evidence, compared with their own previous 3 months (self-reported baseline — weak, noted as such).
- **Retention**: still using it after 8 weeks.

## What would justify continuing (decision rule, set in advance)

Continue if, across at least 10 pilot sellers, ≥ 60 % send 3+ real jobs, ≥ 70 % of their clients approve before paying, and at least half of sellers say they would pay for it. Stop or pivot if clients routinely refuse the approval step.

## Projected benefits (unproven)

Fewer scope disputes, faster evidence assembly, clearer expectations for first-time clients. These are expectations to test, not claims. A short prototype says nothing about chargeback rates.

## Relationship to payment companies

Acquisition or partnership interest from PayPal or any payment company is **unknown**. The hackathon build uses PayPal's public sandbox only and is not endorsed by PayPal.
